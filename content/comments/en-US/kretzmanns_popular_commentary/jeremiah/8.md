# Jeremiah — Chapter 8

Fonte: https://kretzmanncommentary.org/jeremiah/8

### The Calamity of the Jews on Account of Their Persistent Impenitence.

#### Retribution upon the Jews

**[1] At that time, saith the Lord,** namely, when the sentence of God upon Jerusalem would be carried into effect, **they shall bring out the bones of the kings of Judah, and the bones of his princes, and the bones of the priests, and the bones of the prophets,** of all the rulers and leaders of the people, **and the bones of the inhabitants of Jerusalem out of their graves;** [2] **and they shall spread them before the sun and the moon and all the host of heaven,** the stars to whom they had offered worship, **whom they have loved, and whom they have served, and after whom they have walked, and whom they have sought,** turning to them with prayer and appeals for assistance, **and whom they have worshiped; they shall not be gathered nor be buried; they shall be for dung upon the face of the earth,** an example of revolting humiliation. **[3] And death shall be chosen rather than life by all the residue of them that remain of this evil family, which remain in all the places whither I have driven them, saith the Lord of hosts,** for the calamities which would strike them in exile would be so great as to make the survivors consider death a welcome release. Cf. Deut. 28, 26; 30, 1; Lev. 26, 36.

#### The Jews Persist in Wickedness

**[4] Moreover, thou shalt say unto them, Thus saith the Lord, Shall they fall and not arise? Shall he turn away and not return?** or, “Shall a man fall and not get up again? Shall one wander off and not return to the right path?” To do so would certainly be the normal, the natural thing. **[5] Why, then, is this people of Jerusalem slidden back by a perpetual backsliding?** Why do they persist so obstinately in their perverse ways? **They hold fast deceit,** clinging to their hypocritical behavior, **they refuse to return.** **[6] I hearkened and heard,** listening carefully for some evidence of repentance, **but they spake not aright,** they were far from confessing any wrong on their part; **no man repented him of his wickedness, saying, What have I done?** A feeling of guilt, the consciousness of culpability, was absent. **Every one turned to his course,** to the way of his own choosing, **as the horse rusheth into the battle,** an example of headstrong passion. **[7] Yea, the stork in the heaven knoweth her appointed times,** the regular migration seasons; **and the turtle,** that is, the turtle-dove, **and the crane and the swallow observe the time of their coming,** returning with unfailing certainty from their winter homes; **but My people know not the judgment of the Lord,** so that they may apply His norm of judgment to their own case. **[8] How do ye say, We are wise, and the Law of the Lord is with us?** They prided themselves upon their possessing the Law, Rom. 2, 17 ff. **Lo, certainly in vain made He it; the pen of the scribes is in vain,** that is, the lying pen of the scribes, of the false teachers, has changed the Law into a lie, has utterly misrepresented its spirit. **[9] The wise men are ashamed,** put to shame on account of the fact that they are found to be deceivers, **they are dismayed and taken. Lo, they have rejected the Word of the Lord,** setting it aside for their own lies, **and what wisdom is in them?** **[10] Therefore,** on account of the wickedness thus shown by the false teachers, **will I give their wives unto others and their fields to them that shall inherit them; for every one, from the least even unto the greatest, is given to covetousness; from the prophet even unto the priest every one dealeth falsely.** **[11] For they have healed the hurt of the daughter of My people slightly, saying, Peace, peace! when there is no peace.** **[12] Were they ashamed when they had committed abomination? Nay, they were not at all ashamed, neither could they blush; therefore shall they fall among them that fall,** being overthrown with the rest of the sinners, **in the time of their visitation they shall be cast down, saith the Lord.** Thus the behavior of the people of Judah was fully set forth, and the Lord now proceeds to describe the manner in which they are to be punished.

#### Further Description of the Impending Punishment

**[13] I will surely consume them, saith the Lord,** making a clean sweep of them all; **there shall be no grapes on the vine nor figs on the fig-tree,** both of which represented the unfruitful nation, **and the leaf shall fade; and the things that I have given them shall pass away from them,** rather, “I will give them to those who shall overrun them,” the idea of the Lord’s rejection of the Jews being brought out very strongly. **[14] Why do we sit still?** So the prophet hears the people of Judah cry out in view of the approaching calamity. **Assemble yourselves, and let us enter into the defensed cities,** seeking shelter from the invaders in fortified cities, **and let us be silent there,** to perish after but a little respite; **for the Lord, our God, hath put us to silence,** has caused their destruction, **and given us water of gall to drink,** a poisonous draught, **because we have sinned against the Lord,** this realization having come to them when it is too late. **[15] We looked for peace,** still expecting deliverance, **but no good came; and for a time of health,** of healing, **and behold trouble!** Cf. chap. 14, 19. **[16] The snorting of his,** the invader’s, **horses was heard from Dan,** on the northern boundary of Canaan, **the whole land trembled at the sound of the neighing of his strong ones; for they are come and have devoured the land and all that is in it,** the food and the treasures and everything of value, **the city and those that dwell therein.** **[17] For, behold, I will send serpents, cockatrices, among you,** reptiles of a most poisonous kind, **which will not be charmed,** rendered harmless by soothing music, **and they shall bite you, saith the Lord.**

#### The Prophet’s Lament

**[18] When I would comfort myself against sorrow, my heart is faint in me,** literally, “O my exhilaration in sorrow! My heart very ill!” His sorrow was so great that he despaired of finding consolation. **[19] Behold the voice of the cry of the daughter of My people because of them that dwell in a far country,** a lament from the people in exile in distant lands: **Is not the Lord in Zion? Is not her King in her?** It is a question of reproach, as though the people had been delivered into captivity on account of the Lord’s failure to protect them. But He immediately answers with a question on His part: **Why have they provoked Me to anger with their graven images and with strange vanities?** This was the reason why He was compelled to deliver them into the hand of the enemy. **[20] The harvest is past, the summer is ended,** so the people continue to lament, **and we are not saved.** Their hope of deliverance had been vain. **[21] For the hurt of the daughter of my people am I hurt,** so the prophet once more cries out; **I am black,** with deep grief, **astonishment hath taken hold on me,** horror seizing him at the thought of his people’s calamity. **[22] Is there no balm in Gilead?** the country east of the Jordan being famous for its balsam, **is there no physician there? Why, then, is not the health of the daughter of My people recovered?** It was not because there was no healing medicine at hand, for the Word of grace was readily available; it was because the people refused to make use of the remedy offered them in the Lord’s admonitions and promises.
